# Chief Strategy Officer (CSO) Reference & Playbook

## 1. Competitive Advantage: The 7 Powers Framework
Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers define the only durable moats in business:

1. **Scale Economies:** Unit costs decline as volume increases (e.g., Netflix content amortization, AWS data centers).
2. **Network Effects:** Value of the service increases with each additional user (e.g., two-sided marketplaces, communication platforms).
3. **Counter-Positioning:** An upstart adopts a new, superior business model that the incumbent cannot copy without cannibalizing their existing cash cow (e.g., Vanguard vs. active fund managers, Netflix vs. Blockbuster).
4. **Switching Costs:** The pain and financial loss of migrating to an alternative exceeds the incremental value of the competitor (e.g., Epic Systems in EHR, Salesforce in enterprise CRM).
5. **Branding:** Objective consumer trust or status signaling that allows sustained price premiums (e.g., Apple, Tiffany & Co.).
6. **Cornered Resource:** Preferential access to an indispensable asset, patent, or talent pool (e.g., proprietary algorithms, unique mineral rights).
7. **Process Power:** Deeply embedded organizational routines that deliver superior quality or efficiency that cannot be replicated without years of trial-and-error (e.g., Toyota Production System).

> **Operator Rule:** "First-mover advantage" is almost never a power; fast followers routinely capture the market once the pioneer has spent capital educating it.

## 2. Market Sizing & Beachhead Execution
- **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** Total annual revenue opportunity if 100% market share is achieved. Must show a credible path to $100M+ ARR for venture scale.
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** The portion of TAM within your current geographic, regulatory, and channel reach.
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** The realistic wedge you can capture within 24–36 months.
- **Beachhead Rule of Thumb:** Never launch broad. Pick a hyper-specific segment where you can achieve 20-30%+ market share quickly. Win the wedge, build proprietary data/reputation, then expand into adjacent niches.

## 3. Three Horizons of Growth (McKinsey)
- **Horizon 1 (Core Business - 70% Resources):** Defend and optimize the cash-generating core engine. Extends near-term runway and funds exploration.
- **Horizon 2 (Adjacent Bets - 20% Resources):** Emerging lines of business with proven customer interest. Expected to become major revenue drivers in 18–36 months.
- **Horizon 3 (Transformational - 10% Resources):** Long-range R&D, radical business models, or moonshots (3–5 year horizon).

## 4. Build vs. Buy vs. Partner Matrix
| Dimension | Build | Buy (M&A) | Partner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic Core** | Differentiating IP | Accelerating Time-to-Market | Table stakes / non-core |
| **Speed** | Slowest | Fast | Medium |
| **Capital Outlay** | Distributed OpEx | Immediate CapEx / Cash | Low initial CapEx |
| **Integration Risk**| Zero | Extremely High (Culture / Tech) | Low to Medium (Dependency) |

> **Operator Rule:** Never build what can be bought off the shelf unless that specific capability directly creates your competitive moat.
