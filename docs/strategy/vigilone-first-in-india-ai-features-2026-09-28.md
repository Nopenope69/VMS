# VigilOne — "First in India" AI/ML Feature Opportunities, 28 Sept 2026

Companion to `vigilone-ai-features-and-research-2026-09-23.md` (what to copy/buy/avoid, architecturally) and `vigilone-oss-ai-catalog-2026-09-23.md` (licensed model catalog). Those answered "how do we add real AI to VigilOne." This doc answers a narrower question asked directly: **which specific AI/ML features would actually be new to the Indian VMS market**, not just new to VigilOne — based on live research into what Indian vendors ship today, cross-referenced against 2025–2026 papers/repos on video AI.

Method: fetched current feature pages/press for the main Indian players (Videonetics, Staqu JARVIS, Wobot.ai, Vehant, CP Plus×Qualcomm, Matrix×Yotta, Awiros), then searched recent (2025–2026) research literature and GitHub repos for capabilities none of them claim yet. "Confirmed gap" below means: searched specifically for that feature + Indian vendor names and found no product claim; this is evidence of absence, not proof — verify directly with each vendor/a GeM listing before making public "first" claims.

But treat plain NL video search / "AI chat over footage" as a moving target that CP Plus is about to commoditize in India — worth building for parity, not marketing as a "first."

## Part 2 — Candidate features that would genuinely be first in India

Each entry: what it is, why the gap looks real, the paper/repo to start from, and the catch.

### 1. Zero-shot, natural-language-*defined* anomaly rules (not just NL search over recordings)
**What**: an operator types "alert me if someone climbs the back fence after 10pm" or "flag anyone loitering near the ATM for more than 3 minutes" and the system starts detecting it immediately — no labelled training data, no per-site model.
**Why it's different from what CP Plus/Videonetics claim**: their NL features are *retrospective search* ("find the clip where…") and generic *event correlation*. This is *prospective, user-customizable detection logic* defined in plain language — directly useful for India's long tail of small, idiosyncratic sites (a single shop, a specific gate) that no vendor can afford to hand-model.
**Sources**: [AnyAnomaly: Zero-Shot Customizable Video Anomaly Detection with LVLM (WACV 2026)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Ahn_AnyAnomaly_Zero-Shot_Customizable_Video_Anomaly_Detection_with_LVLM_WACV_2026_paper.pdf), [PANDA: Generalist Video Anomaly Detection via Agentic AI Engineer (2025)](https://github.com/mala-lab/Awesome-Anomaly-Detection-Foundation-Models), [Language-guided Open-world VAD (2025)](https://github.com/mala-lab/Awesome-Anomaly-Detection-Foundation-Models).
**Catch**: a dedicated reality-check paper this session found gives a sober number — [Are Multimodal LLMs Ready for Surveillance? (arXiv 2603.04727)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04727v1) reports baseline F1 of **0.09**, rising to only **0.64** with heavy prompt engineering, with a "conservative bias" causing recall collapse (it misses real anomalies more than it should). This is consistent with the Sept 23 doc's own VLM caveat (27–35% on UCF-Crime). **Launch this as an operator-assist triage layer that surfaces candidates for human review, never as an autonomous alarm**, and say so publicly — that honesty is itself a differentiator per the existing "no benchmark, no pilot, it's a slide" positioning.

### 2. Predictive crowd-crush / bottleneck forecasting (not threshold density alerts)
**What**: forecast *where and when* a dangerous crowd crush is forming a few minutes ahead, from flow vectors and bottleneck geometry — not just alarm once density crosses a fixed number.
**Why it's different**: confirmed this session that both Awiros (Telangana) and the Kumbh Mela command centre's public description are threshold/reactive — density in, alarm out. No Indian vendor described predictive flow modelling.
**Why it matters for India specifically**: recurring, high-fatality crowd-crush events (Kumbh Mela stampedes, temple gatherings, railway platforms, political rallies) make this a real, well-publicized need rather than a speculative feature.
**Sources**: general crowd-dynamics/social-force forecasting research (search "pedestrian flow prediction stampede risk" for the current literature — not independently verified this session beyond the news coverage of [Kumbh Mela's threshold-based system](https://www.forbesindia.com/article/lifes/maha-kumbh-mela-how-ai-is-helping-stop-stampedes-at-worlds-biggest-gathering/95122/1) and [Telangana's Awiros deployment](https://indiaai.gov.in/article/how-telangana-is-averting-stampedes-through-efficient-crowd-monitoring)).
**Catch**: needs dense camera coverage with overlapping fields of view and calibrated homography to get real flow vectors — a heavier lift than a single-camera feature. Pilot at one high-risk site type (a railway platform or a temple gate) rather than as a general v1 claim.

### 3. Gait-based re-identification for occluded/masked faces
**What**: re-identify or track a person across cameras by *how they walk*, when the face is hidden by a helmet, mask, or hood.
**Why it's different**: every Indian vendor found does face-based or attribute-based tracking; none market gait. This is unusually relevant in India specifically because two-wheeler helmet laws and face-covering (dupatta, mask) are common street conditions that defeat face-based re-ID.
**Signal that this is a live interest, not a stretch**: [Indian police used gait biometrics in a July 2026 Pune murder investigation](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/indian-police-turn-to-gait-biometrics-in-high-profile-murder-investigation) (vendor not disclosed in coverage), and IIT Delhi has a *seismic* (floor-vibration, not camera) gait biometrics line in patent phase — different sensing modality, same underlying interest.
**Source to build from**: [OpenGait (ShiqiYu/OpenGait, CVPR 2023 origin)](https://github.com/ShiqiYu/OpenGait) — MIT-licensed, actively maintained framework, the standard open baseline for camera-based gait recognition.
**Catch**: gait recognition accuracy is known to be sensitive to viewpoint, clothing, carried objects, and walking speed (see the gait-recognition review literature). Position as a forensic/investigative add-on for flagged cases, not a real-time default identification layer.

### 4. Audio-visual fused threat detection (gunshot, scream, glass-break)
**What**: fuse acoustic event detection with the visual context of the same moment to catch and locate threats sound reveals before anything visible happens (a shot fired off-camera, breaking glass, a scream).
**Why it's different**: confirmed gap — no Indian VMS/CCTV vendor found offering audio analytics as a shipped product feature. Only international players (Axis Sound Detection, Avigilon Gunshot Audio Analytics, ShotSpotter) do this, and an Indian trade-press piece on gunshot detection ([securityupdate.in](https://securityupdate.in/gunshot-detection-technology-hearing-is-believing/)) covers it purely as a foreign technology, with no Indian deployment mentioned.
**Sources**: [AVadCLIP (2025) — audio-visual collaborative anomaly detection](https://github.com/mala-lab/Awesome-Anomaly-Detection-Foundation-Models), classical acoustic-event papers (spectrogram CNNs for gunshot/scream classification — several 2025 papers surfaced this session, e.g. deep spectrogram gunshot classification comparative studies).
**Catch**: needs microphone-equipped cameras or separate audio ingestion, which VigilOne's pipeline doesn't currently have (the Sept 23 catalog flagged this: "only worthwhile if your camera streams actually carry audio"). Realistic as a v2+ feature for a specific vertical (banks, schools, VIP security) rather than a general-purpose v1 claim. Audio recording has stricter privacy law than video — keep opt-in per the existing catalog's note.

### 5. Verbalized, evidence-chained AI reasoning ("why the AI flagged this," cryptographically logged)
**What**: every AI-raised alert carries a plain-language explanation of *why* the model flagged it, generated by the same verification step, and that explanation is hashed into the tamper-evident evidence chain alongside the detection.
**Why it's different**: this isn't a new detector, it's a synthesis of two things VigilOne is uniquely positioned to combine — the Sept 23 doc's own conclusion that "the strongest foundation for trustworthy AI later" is VigilOne's *already-real* Section 63 BSA tamper-evident audit/evidence chain, plus this session's finding that no Indian vendor markets AI explainability tied to legal evidence. Competitors have the AI features; none have the evidence-chain foundation to make the AI's reasoning admissible and durable.
**Source**: [VERA — Explainable Video Anomaly Detection via Verbalized Learning of Vision-Language Models (2025)](https://github.com/mala-lab/Awesome-Anomaly-Detection-Foundation-Models).
**Catch**: only meaningful once the AI Adapter Contract and a real detector exist (Sept 23 doc's recommended path items 1–2). Cheap to add on top of that work rather than a separate R&D track — arguably the **best first "first"** to ship, because it compounds an asset no competitor has rather than requiring new model R&D.

### 6. Weapon (gun/knife) detection as a mainstream, licensed VMS feature
**What**: real-time firearm/edged-weapon detection integrated into the standard analytics package, not a bolt-on security-only product.
**Why it's different**: confirmed gap — none of Videonetics, Staqu, Vehant, Wobot, or CP Plus list weapon detection (Videonetics has "fight/aggression detection," which is behavioral, not object-based). Only Western niche vendors (ZeroEyes, Omnilert, Athena Security, Evolv) do this, and none surfaced as active in the Indian VMS market this session.
**Sources**: [AI-Based Weapon Detection for Security Surveillance: Recent Research Advances 2016–2025 (MDPI, 2025 survey)](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/14/23/4609); open reference implementations exist (e.g. [RunzeXU/AI-detection-weapons](https://github.com/RunzeXU/AI-detection-weapons)) but check each one's licence individually — the Sept 23 catalog's dataset-minefield warning applies directly here (public weapon-detection datasets like ACF and various Roboflow sets have mixed/unclear commercial licensing; plan to fine-tune a permissively-licensed detector such as RF-DETR/YOLOX on cleared data rather than importing a pretrained weapon model wholesale).
**Catch**: false-positive tolerance for weapon alerts must be much lower than for generic object detection — an unvalidated model here is a liability, not just an embarrassment. Needs the same "publish precision/recall on your own footage before claiming it" discipline the Sept 23 doc already recommends for every AI claim.

## Part 3 — What NOT to lead with as a "first"

These are already commoditized among Indian vendors (confirmed this session) — build them for parity/checkbox reasons, not as the differentiating pitch:
- Natural-language video search / "chat with your footage" — Videonetics claims it, CP Plus×Qualcomm is shipping it Q1 2026.
- Crowd density counting/alerting (threshold-based) — Awiros, Videonetics.
- PPE compliance, fire/smoke detection, ANPR, helmet/seatbelt/phone-while-driving — Staqu, Vehant, CP Plus all ship these.
- Basic people/vehicle/object detection and dashboards — table stakes (Wobot, everyone).

## Part 4 — How this fits the existing roadmap

Per the "status vs MVP/north star" note already in project memory, VigilOne currently has **no working ML inference at all** (per the 23/27 Sept audits) — an ai-worker scaffold exists but no model file is loaded. The honest sequencing is: ship Phase 2 of the existing north-star route (RF-DETR/YOLOX detector + tracker + published precision/recall — the credibility baseline) before claiming any "first." Once that lands, item 5 (verbalized evidence-chained reasoning) is the cheapest and most defensible "first" to ship next — it rides on VigilOne's already-real audit chain rather than needing new model R&D — followed by item 1 (zero-shot NL anomaly rules, framed explicitly as operator-assist, not autonomous). Items 2, 3, 4 and 6 need more data, hardware, or vertical-specific investment and fit later phases (India ANPR/redaction phase, semantic search phase, and beyond) rather than v1.

## Not verified / caveats
- Vendor feature lists come from each company's own marketing pages, not from hands-on trials, RFP responses, or GeM listings — verify directly before making competitive claims in a pitch or grant document.
- CP Plus × Qualcomm's Q1 2026 launch is an announcement, not a confirmed shipped product as of this writing (28 Sept 2026) — worth rechecking closer to any VigilOne launch date, since it's the single biggest moving target in this analysis.
- "Confirmed gap" claims (weapon detection, audio analytics, gait re-ID) are absence-of-evidence from targeted searches this session, not exhaustive competitive intelligence — a smaller/regional Indian vendor could already offer one of these without strong web presence.
- Crowd-crush predictive-forecasting research wasn't pinned to a single canonical paper this session (general social-force/flow-prediction literature exists but wasn't individually verified) — treat Part 2 item 2's sourcing as a direction to research further, not a specific implementation to copy.
- Staqu's police-facial-recognition scale claims are background knowledge, not verified this session.
